Statistical Anomaly Detection: EWMA, Modified Z-score and CUSUM

A fixed threshold rule, such as flagging a task whose run time exceeds 1.5 times its expected burst time, is simple but arbitrary: it ignores how variable the task normally is, fires on single noisy values, and gives no principled way to choose the threshold. Statistical process control (SPC) methods instead compare a new value against the observed behaviour of a baseline, and they are the same family of methods behind the anomaly bands in Prometheus, Grafana and AWS CloudWatch.

The Exponentially Weighted Moving Average (EWMA) control chart, as specified by the NIST/SEMATECH Engineering Statistics Handbook, smooths a series with EWMA_t = lambda * Y_t + (1 - lambda) * EWMA_(t-1). Control limits are EWMA_0 plus or minus k * s_ewma, where s_ewma^2 = (lambda / (2 - lambda)) * s^2. Conventional choices are lambda between 0.2 and 0.3 and k = 3. EWMA is good at detecting small, sustained shifts.

The Modified Z-score uses the median and the median absolute deviation (MAD) instead of the mean and standard deviation: M_i = 0.6745 * (x_i - median) / MAD, and a value is flagged as an outlier when the absolute modified Z-score exceeds 3.5. NIST recommends it over the naive Z-score rule (flag when |Z| is greater than 2 or 3) because the mean and standard deviation are themselves distorted by the outliers being detected, whereas the median and MAD are robust to them.

CUSUM, the cumulative sum control chart, detects a persistent shift by accumulating deviations from a target: S_hi = max(0, S_hi_prev + (x_i - target - k)) and S_lo = max(0, S_lo_prev - (x_i - target + k)). A shift is flagged when either sum crosses a decision interval h. The reference value k and interval h can be derived from the smallest shift worth detecting and the acceptable false-alarm and miss rates.

A known caveat when applying SPC to IT systems is that the stable-mean assumption often fails for bursty workloads, which causes false positives. A rolling baseline window that re-baselines over recent values reduces this problem.

Procura's detector keeps a rolling window of the last 30 ratios of actual time to expected burst time for each task. It supports four selectable methods: ewma (default, lambda 0.25, k 3), modified_zscore (threshold 3.5), cusum (shift of 1 sigma, decision interval of 5 sigma), and the original fixed_threshold rule, kept only for comparison. Memory-allocation failures and deadlocks are detected directly, not statistically: a memory request larger than available memory, and a cycle in the wait-for graph.

Production AIOps platforms follow the same two-layer pattern: statistical detection of anomalies first, then correlation and root-cause diagnosis. Datadog Watchdog provides always-on anomaly detection plus a root cause analysis feature, Dynatrace Davis AI uses causal fault-tree analysis over topology data, and New Relic combines proactive detection with incident correlation.
